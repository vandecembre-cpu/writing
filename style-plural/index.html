A style workbook in seven steps

Style, plural.

You are more than one person in a week. Your closet should know that.

This is not a quiz that hands you a label. It is a way to understand the different versions of you, dress the ones you actually live as, and shop on purpose.

You're looking at sample answers. They show how each step works. Clear them to fill in your own.

1

What is style?

Style is how you say who you are before you say anything at all. It lives in the way you put things together, what you reach for when no one is watching, and how you want to feel when you walk out the door.

There is no correct answer. Trends tell you what everyone is wearing. Style is what you keep choosing, and why.

Personal

It belongs to your body, your budget, your climate and your days. Someone else's rules can inspire you, but they can't define you.

Ever-changing

A new job, a new city, a new decade or a new mood will change what feels right. That is growth, not inconsistency.

Plural

You are a colleague, a friend, a partner, a weekend wanderer. Each version of you gets dressed a little differently.

Pop quiz: three versions of you

Think of three different versions of yourself. Maybe it's the you who goes to work, the you who runs errands on Saturday, and the you who gets dressed up for a night out. Maybe it's the parent, the artist and the traveler. Give each one a name and describe how they dress.

Stuck? Try: Weekday you, Weekend you, Evening you, Travel you, Creative you, Future you.

3

Shop your closet first

Before you buy anything new, look at what you already own. Pull everything out, try it on, and ask four questions of every piece. You are looking for the clothes that make you look good and feel good. Those are your blueprint.

Do I look good?Check it in the mirror, in daylight.
Do I feel like myself?Or do you feel like you're in a costume?
Does it fit today?Your body now, not a past or future one.
Worn in the last year?A full year covers every season.
4 yeses = keep. 2 or 3 = maybe. 0 or 1 = let it go. You can always override.

4

Let go: sell, donate, or rehome

Everything that didn't earn a keep gets a next home. Letting go makes room to see what you actually love, and selling puts money back into your budget for step 7.

Your let-go pile

Choose where each piece goes. Add an asking price for anything you sell.

The maybe box

Put your maybes in a box and write today's date on it. Anything you haven't gone looking for in 30 days goes to the let-go pile.

Sell

Best for pieces in great condition, recognizable labels, or items with tags still on. Photograph in daylight, list measurements, and price about a third of retail.

Donate

Clean, wearable pieces that won't sell quickly. Check what local shelters and charities actually need before you drop off.

Swap

Host a clothing swap with friends. Everyone leaves with something new and nobody spends a thing.

Tailor it

If you love it but the fit is off, a hem or a nip at the waist is often cheaper than replacing it.

Recycle textiles

Stained, torn or worn-through pieces go to a textile recycling drop-off, not the trash.

Who have you been shopping for?

Now compare how you actually spend your week with who your closet is dressed for. Most of us shop for the version of ourselves that's the most fun to picture, not the one who gets dressed most mornings. Pieces bought for "someday" land under Someone else.

6

Add with intent

Now you know what makes you feel good and which version of you is under-dressed. Build a short list of what to add. A strong addition works with at least three things you already own and serves a version of you that actually needs it.

7

Set your budget

Decide what you can spend before you open a single shopping tab. Then split it the way you split your week, so the version of you who gets dressed most often gets most of the money.
